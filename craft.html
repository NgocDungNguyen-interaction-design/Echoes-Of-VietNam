NÓN LÁ

THE ART OF THE VIETNAMESE CONICAL HAT

Nón Lá

A simple creation with a timeless identity.

The Story Behind the Craft

The nón lá, or Vietnamese conical hat, is one of the most recognisable traditional objects in Vietnam.

For generations, it has been part of everyday life, particularly in agricultural communities where people needed protection from the sun and rain. Its lightweight structure and practical shape made it suitable for working in rice fields, travelling and carrying out daily activities.

Traditional hat-making has developed in different regions of Vietnam. Villages such as Chuông in Northern Vietnam and Vân Thê in Huế are known for preserving this craft.

Materials

  • Palm leaves – the main material forming the outer surface.
  • Bamboo – used to create the circular frame.
  • Thread – used to sew the leaves together.
  • Fabric or silk – used for the chin strap and decoration.

How It Is Made

  1. Selecting the leaves: Suitable palm leaves are collected and dried.
  2. Preparing the leaves: The leaves are carefully flattened using heat.
  3. Making the frame: Thin bamboo strips are shaped into concentric circular rings.
  4. Layering: The prepared leaves are arranged over the bamboo frame.
  5. Stitching: Artisans carefully sew the leaves together using fine thread.
  6. Finishing: The edges are trimmed, and a chin strap is attached.

Cultural Significance

Beyond its practical purpose, the nón lá has become an important symbol of Vietnamese cultural identity. It is frequently associated with the image of Vietnamese women wearing áo dài, but the hat is traditionally used by people of different ages and backgrounds. Its simple design represents the resourcefulness and craftsmanship of Vietnamese communities.

Did You Know?

Huế is famous for its nón bài thơ (poem conical hat), which contains decorative images or verses that become visible when the hat is held against the light.

TRANH DÂN GIAN

THE ART OF VIETNAMESE FOLK PAINTINGS

Tranh Dân Gian

Stories and beliefs printed in colour.

The Story Behind the Craft

Vietnamese folk paintings are a traditional art form that reflects the beliefs, everyday life and cultural values of local communities.

Among the best-known styles is Đông Hồ folk painting, originating from Đông Hồ village in Bắc Ninh. The tradition developed over several centuries and became particularly associated with the preparation for Tết, the Vietnamese Lunar New Year.

Unlike paintings created entirely by hand, Đông Hồ prints are traditionally produced using carved wooden printing blocks. Their subjects include animals, farming activities, festivals, historical stories and symbolic images representing good fortune.

Materials

  • Giấy điệp: Traditional paper coated with powdered seashell, creating a subtle shimmering surface.
  • Wooden blocks – carved blocks used to print outlines and colours.
  • Natural pigments – colours traditionally prepared from natural materials such as plants, minerals and other sources.
  • Printing tools – brushes and traditional tools used to apply pigments.

How It Is Made

  1. Preparing the paper: Traditional dó paper is coated with a mixture containing ground seashell powder.
  2. Designing the image: The artist creates an illustration based on traditional themes.
  3. Carving the blocks: Designs are carved in reverse onto wooden printing blocks.
  4. Preparing the colours: Natural pigments are mixed and prepared for printing.
  5. Printing: Each colour is applied using a separate carved block, building the image layer by layer.
  6. Finishing: The final black outline is printed to complete the artwork.

Cultural Significance

Đông Hồ paintings are more than decorative artworks. They reflect Vietnamese beliefs, social values, humour and everyday experiences. Traditionally, families displayed these colourful prints during Tết as symbols of happiness, prosperity and good fortune. Today, the craft remains an important part of Vietnamese cultural heritage, although the number of practising artisans has become limited.

Did You Know?

In 2025, the craft of making Đông Hồ folk woodblock prints was inscribed on UNESCO's List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding.

GỐM

THE ART OF VIETNAMESE POTTERY

Gốm

Shaping the earth into everyday beauty.

The Story Behind the Craft

Vietnamese pottery has a long history, with traditional ceramic villages developing around areas rich in clay and other natural resources.

One of the most famous examples is Bát Tràng Ceramic Village, located near Hà Nội. The village has been recognised as an important centre of Vietnamese pottery for centuries. Its artisans developed techniques for shaping, decorating and glazing ceramics, producing objects for both everyday use and decorative purposes.

Traditional Bát Tràng ceramics include bowls, plates, vases, tea sets and decorative artworks.

Materials

  • Clay – the primary material used to shape ceramic objects.
  • Kaolin – a type of clay used in ceramic production.
  • Water – used to prepare and shape the clay.
  • Glazes – applied to create different colours and surface finishes.
  • Natural pigments – used for decorative patterns.

How It Is Made

  1. Preparing the clay: Clay is cleaned, processed and kneaded to remove air bubbles and impurities.
  2. Shaping: Artisans use a pottery wheel or moulds to create the desired form.
  3. Drying: The shaped object is left to dry before further treatment.
  4. Decorating: Patterns and illustrations are painted or carved onto the surface.
  5. Glazing: A layer of glaze is applied to create colour and protect the ceramic.
  6. Firing: The ceramic is placed inside a kiln and fired at high temperatures to become hard and durable.

Cultural Significance

Pottery reflects the relationship between Vietnamese communities and the natural materials surrounding them. In Bát Tràng, ceramic production has become an important part of local identity and economic life. The craft also demonstrates how traditional techniques can continue to develop through contemporary design and artistic experimentation.

Did You Know?

Traditional Bát Tràng ceramics are known for their distinctive glaze techniques, including blue-and-white decoration and crackle glaze.

DỆT MAY

THE ART OF VIETNAMESE TEXTILES

Dệt May

Threads that carry culture and identity.

The Story Behind the Craft

Textile weaving is an important part of Vietnam's traditional craftsmanship. Across the country, different communities have developed their own weaving techniques, materials and decorative patterns.

One well-known example is Vạn Phúc Silk Village, located in Hà Nội. The village is recognised for its traditional silk production, which has been maintained and developed across generations.

Vietnam is also home to many ethnic communities with distinctive weaving traditions. Their textiles often feature geometric patterns, symbolic motifs and colours connected to local identities and cultural beliefs.

From silk garments to handwoven brocade, Vietnamese textiles demonstrate the diversity of the country's traditional craft heritage.

Materials

  • Silk – used to produce smooth and lightweight fabrics.
  • Cotton – commonly used for everyday textiles.
  • Natural fibres – depending on the region and local resources.
  • Natural dyes – traditionally obtained from plants and other natural materials.
  • Wooden looms – used to weave threads into fabric.

How It Is Made

  1. Preparing the fibres: Raw silk, cotton or other fibres are cleaned and prepared.
  2. Spinning: Fibres are processed into threads.
  3. Dyeing: Threads are coloured using traditional or modern dyeing techniques.
  4. Setting up the loom: Threads are arranged vertically to form the warp.
  5. Weaving: Horizontal threads are interlaced with the warp to create fabric.
  6. Finishing: The finished textile is cleaned, dried and prepared for use.

Cultural Significance

Vietnamese textile traditions represent more than the production of fabric. They preserve the knowledge, identities and artistic expressions of different communities. Patterns and weaving techniques are often passed down through generations, connecting traditional craftsmanship with contemporary fashion, design and cultural expression.

Did You Know?

Different Vietnamese ethnic communities have distinctive textile traditions. Their woven patterns can communicate cultural identity, local history and traditional beliefs.
