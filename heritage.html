EXPLORE VIETNAMESE CULTURE

Where History Lives On

Discover the heritage that shapes Vietnam.

Vietnam's heritage is a reflection of its history, beliefs, architecture and natural landscapes. From ancient royal citadels and historic trading towns to sacred temples and breathtaking natural wonders, each place preserves a part of the country's story.

Explore five remarkable heritage sites and discover the history, people and cultural values that have shaped Vietnam through generations.

IMPERIAL CITY OF HUẾ

THE LEGACY OF VIETNAM'S LAST IMPERIAL DYNASTY

Imperial City of Huế

A royal capital shaped by ceremony and tradition.

LocationHuế, Central VietnamUNESCO recognition1993

History & Origins

Huế became the imperial capital of Vietnam in 1802, when Emperor Gia Long established the Nguyễn Dynasty. For more than a century, the city served as the political, cultural and religious centre of the country.

The Imperial City was constructed as the heart of the royal capital, following principles of traditional Vietnamese architecture and geomancy, with influences from East Asian imperial planning.

The complex includes palaces, temples, ceremonial gates and the Forbidden Purple City, which was once reserved for the emperor and royal household.

What Makes It Special?

  • Ngọ Môn Gate: The main ceremonial entrance to the Imperial City.
  • Thái Hòa Palace: A major venue for royal ceremonies.
  • Forbidden Purple City: The former private residence of the emperor.
  • Royal Temples: Places dedicated to the worship of Nguyễn Dynasty emperors.

Cultural Significance

The Imperial City of Huế preserves the history and traditions of Vietnam's last monarchy. Its architecture, ceremonial spaces and royal heritage offer a glimpse into the country's imperial past.

02

HỘI AN ANCIENT TOWN

WHERE CULTURES MEET AND HISTORY REMAINS

Hội An Ancient Town

A meeting place of trade, culture and tradition.

LocationHội An, Central VietnamUNESCO recognition1999

History & Origins

Hội An was once an important international trading port in Southeast Asia, particularly between the 15th and 19th centuries.

Merchants from Japan, China and other regions travelled to the town to exchange goods and establish commercial relationships.

This international trade contributed to the development of Hội An's distinctive architecture and cultural identity, combining local Vietnamese traditions with influences from different communities.

What Makes It Special?

  • Japanese Covered Bridge: One of the town's most recognisable historical structures.
  • Ancient Merchant Houses: Traditional wooden buildings reflecting centuries of trade.
  • Assembly Halls: Religious and communal spaces built by Chinese merchant communities.
  • Lantern Traditions: Colourful lanterns that have become an iconic feature of Hội An.

Cultural Significance

Hội An represents the cultural exchange that shaped Vietnam's coastal trading communities. Its preserved streets and architecture demonstrate how different cultures interacted while maintaining their own identities.

03

MỸ SƠN SANCTUARY

THE SACRED REMAINS OF CHAMPA CIVILISATION

Mỹ Sơn Sanctuary

A sacred landscape shaped by the Champa civilisation.

LocationQuảng Nam, Central VietnamUNESCO recognition1999

History & Origins

Mỹ Sơn was an important religious and ceremonial centre of the ancient Champa civilisation.

The temple complex developed between the 4th and 13th centuries and was closely associated with Hindu beliefs, particularly the worship of Shiva.

The site contains numerous brick temple towers surrounded by mountains and forest. These structures demonstrate the architectural and artistic achievements of the Cham people.

What Makes It Special?

  • Brick Temple Towers: Structures built using distinctive construction techniques.
  • Hindu Sculptures: Decorative carvings depicting religious figures and symbols.
  • Shiva Worship: Evidence of the spiritual beliefs of the Champa civilisation.
  • Cham Architecture: Distinctive forms and ornamentation reflecting regional artistic traditions.

Cultural Significance

Mỹ Sơn preserves an important part of Vietnam's multicultural history. It reminds visitors that Vietnamese heritage includes the contributions of many different civilisations and communities.

04

THĂNG LONG IMPERIAL CITADEL

A THOUSAND YEARS OF VIETNAMESE HISTORY

Thăng Long Imperial Citadel

Layers of history at the heart of the capital.

LocationHà Nội, Northern VietnamUNESCO recognition2010

History & Origins

The Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long was established in 1010, when King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital from Hoa Lư to Đại La and renamed it Thăng Long.

For many centuries, the citadel served as the political centre of successive Vietnamese dynasties.

Archaeological discoveries have revealed layers of historical remains, including foundations of palaces, ancient structures, ceramics and other artefacts from different periods.

What Makes It Special?

  • Đoan Môn Gate: A historic entrance to the central royal area.
  • Hanoi Flag Tower: A prominent landmark associated with the citadel.
  • Archaeological Remains: Evidence of successive dynasties and historical periods.
  • Kinh Thiên Palace Foundations: Remains of an important royal ceremonial structure.

Cultural Significance

Thăng Long Imperial Citadel represents more than a thousand years of Vietnamese political and cultural history. It reflects the development of the country's royal institutions and the continuity of its capital.

05

HẠ LONG BAY

A NATURAL WONDER SHAPED BY TIME

Hạ Long Bay

A dramatic landscape shaped by nature and legend.

LocationQuảng Ninh, Northern VietnamUNESCO recognition1994; extended in 2023

History & Origins

Hạ Long Bay is one of Vietnam's most remarkable natural landscapes, famous for its emerald waters and thousands of limestone islands and islets.

The bay was shaped over millions of years through geological processes, including the development and erosion of limestone formations.

Its landscape contains caves, hidden lagoons and distinctive rock formations, creating one of the most recognisable coastal environments in Southeast Asia.

What Makes It Special?

  • Limestone Karsts: Dramatic rock formations rising from the sea.
  • Hidden Caves: Natural caves formed through geological processes.
  • Islands and Lagoons: Diverse coastal landscapes and ecosystems.
  • Traditional Fishing Communities: A long connection between local communities and the sea.

Cultural Significance

Although primarily recognised for its natural value, Hạ Long Bay is also connected to local livelihoods, traditional fishing communities and Vietnamese folklore.

Its name, meaning Descending Dragon, is associated with a legend about dragons descending from the heavens to protect the land from invaders.
